Tokens on Solana

You have 100 USDC in your wallet. Where are they, exactly? The short answer is that they are not inside the wallet at all, and understanding why explains how every token on Solana works.

  • levelbeginner
  • prerequisitesbasic Solana concepts (accounts, programs)
  • reading time~6 min
  • facts verified18 Nov 2026

01What a token is

A token is a digital asset that lives on a blockchain: a dollar stablecoin like USDC, points in a game, shares in a project or a ticket to an event. On Solana, tokens are often called SPL tokens, after the Solana Program Library where the programs that manage them were first published.

A token does not travel from wallet to wallet like a file. It is a record kept by a program, the Token Program, and the easiest way to picture it is a spreadsheet. The top of the sheet describes the token, and each row below says who holds how much.

On Solana both parts are accounts. The description of the token is the mint account, and each row is a token account. Sending tokens means asking the Token Program to subtract from one row and add to another; nothing else moves.

02The mint account

Every token has exactly one mint account, and its address is how the token is identified. Two tokens can share a name or a logo, but never a mint address, which is why wallets and exchanges always check it. USDC on Solana, for example, is the mint EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v.

The mint stores four pieces of information:

FieldWhat it means
supplyHow many units exist in total, the sum of every token account of this mint.
decimalsHow many parts one unit can be split into (section 04).
mint authorityThe address allowed to create new units, if any.
freeze authorityThe address allowed to freeze token accounts, if any.

The mint holds no balances itself. It only defines the token and keeps count of how many units exist.

03Token accounts

A token account holds the balance of one token for one owner. Its main fields are the mint it belongs to, the owner who can move the funds, and the amount. Because each token account belongs to a single mint, a wallet that holds three different tokens has three token accounts, plus its regular account for SOL.

So the answer to the opening question is this: your 100 USDC are recorded in a token account whose owner is your wallet. The wallet holds the private key that authorizes moving them, and that is why losing the key means losing the tokens, even though they were never stored on your device.

Associated token accounts

If anyone could open token accounts at random addresses, sending tokens would require asking the recipient which account to use. To avoid that, there is a standard address for each pair of wallet and token, the associated token account. It is calculated from the wallet address and the mint address, so anyone can work out where your USDC account is from your wallet address alone. When you send tokens to a wallet, your wallet app calculates this address and uses it.

Creating an account costs a refundable deposit

Every account on Solana takes up space on every validator, so it must hold a minimum balance of SOL, proportional to its size, called rent. Despite the name, it works as a deposit rather than a fee: when the account is closed, the SOL is returned in full. For a token account it amounts to a few cents or less, and the network has been reducing it in stages during 2026.

This is why the first time someone receives a token they have never held, somebody has to pay to create their token account. Usually it is the sender, and wallet apps do it automatically.

04Decimals

Token accounts store amounts as whole numbers, never as fractions. To allow amounts like 25.5, each mint declares how many decimal places its token uses, and the number stored is the amount multiplied by ten to that power. USDC uses 6 decimals, so 100 USDC is stored as 100,000,000, and 0.01 USDC is stored as 10,000.

DecimalsStored value 1,000,000 meansTypical use

Wallets and explorers divide by the right power of ten before showing you the amount, so you rarely see the stored number. It matters when you write code: sending "100" to a program that expects stored units would send 0.0001 USDC.

05Authorities: who can create and freeze

The two authorities stored in the mint decide how much control its creator keeps over the token.

The mint authority can create new units at any time and assign them to any token account, which raises the supply. The freeze authority can freeze any token account of that mint, and a frozen account can neither send nor receive the token until the authority unfreezes it.

Each authority can be handed to another address or removed for good. Removing the mint authority fixes the supply forever, since nobody can create more units afterwards, and many projects do exactly that to show their holders that the supply cannot be inflated. Removing the freeze authority guarantees that no account will ever be blocked.

USDC keeps both. Circle creates new USDC when someone deposits dollars with it and removes USDC when someone redeems them, so it needs the mint authority. It keeps the freeze authority to comply with legal orders, which is how it can freeze the USDC held at a specific address. Before trusting a token, it is worth checking who holds these two authorities: any explorer shows them on the token's page.

06Two token programs

Solana has two programs that manage tokens. The original Token Program covers the basics described so far. The newer Token Extensions Program, also known as Token-2022, does the same and adds optional features, called extensions, that a token enables when it is created:

ExtensionWhat it does
metadataStores the name, symbol and image link inside the mint itself.
transfer feeKeeps a percentage of every transfer for the token's creator.
non-transferableTokens cannot be moved once received, useful for certificates or badges.
default account stateNew token accounts start frozen until the issuer approves them.
confidential transfersHides transfer amounts from everyone except the parties involved.

The two programs have different addresses, TokenkegQfeZyiNwAJbNbGKPFXCWuBvf9Ss623VQ5DA for the original and TokenzQdBNbLqP5VEhdkAS6EPFLC1PHnBqCXEpPxuEb for Token-2022, and every mint belongs to one of them. Most established tokens, USDC included, use the original program.

07Summary

A token on Solana is a record kept by a token program. Its mint account defines it, with its supply, its decimals and its two authorities, and each holder's balance lives in a separate token account owned by their wallet. Your wallet never contains the tokens; it holds the key that allows you to move them.
